# Fiscal Stress Analysis in Kerala (2005–2025)

## Overview

A data-driven analysis of Kerala's fiscal performance and fiscal stress from 2005–06 to 2024–25. The study examines revenue mobilisation, government expenditure, fiscal deficits, economic growth, and fiscal sustainability using secondary data from the Economic Review published by the Government of Kerala.

The project evaluates long-term fiscal trends and identifies periods of relatively high and low fiscal stress using a composite Fiscal Stress Score.

## Objectives

- To examine trends in Kerala's major fiscal indicators from 2005–06 to 2024–25.
- To analyze relationships among major fiscal variables using correlation analysis.
- To compare fiscal performance across Pre-GST, GST, COVID-19, and Recovery periods.
- To evaluate the growth patterns of key fiscal indicators.
- To assess Kerala's fiscal condition using a Fiscal Stress Score.

## Key Findings

- Own Tax Revenue and Revenue Receipts showed a consistent upward trend over the study period.
- Revenue Expenditure and Capital Expenditure increased substantially over time.
- Revenue and Fiscal Deficits showed significant increases, with pronounced pressure during the COVID-19 period.
- The COVID period recorded the highest expenditure and deficit ratios.
- The Recovery period showed improvement in fiscal conditions, with declining deficit ratios and strengthening revenue performance.
- Strong positive relationships were observed among major fiscal variables.
- Revenue Receipts and Total Expenditure showed an extremely strong positive correlation.
- The Fiscal Stress Score identified **2020–21 as the year with the highest fiscal stress**.
- **2014–15 recorded the most favourable fiscal position** during the study period.
- Recent years showed signs of gradual restoration of fiscal stability.

## Fiscal Stress Score

The Fiscal Stress Score was constructed using standardized values of:

- Revenue Receipt Ratio
- Revenue Expenditure Ratio
- Revenue Deficit Ratio

Lower, more negative scores indicate relatively higher fiscal stress, while higher positive scores indicate comparatively better fiscal conditions.

The highest fiscal stress was recorded in **2020–21**, with a score of **−0.6311**, primarily reflecting the fiscal impact of the COVID-19 pandemic.

## Key Insights

The analysis indicates that Kerala experienced substantial long-term growth in revenue mobilisation, government expenditure, and GSDP. However, major economic disruptions placed significant pressure on the state's fiscal position.

The COVID-19 period represented the most severe fiscal stress, while subsequent recovery years showed gradual improvement in fiscal stability.

## Conclusion

The study highlights the importance of strengthening revenue mobilisation, maintaining expenditure discipline, increasing productive capital expenditure, and developing fiscal buffers to improve Kerala's long-term fiscal sustainability.

The Fiscal Stress Score provides a useful composite measure for identifying changes in fiscal pressure across different economic conditions.
